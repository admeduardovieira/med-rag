# 1. Code Structure

## Diagnosis codes:

- 3 to 5 digits.

- The first digit may be alpha (E or V) or numeric.
- Digits 2–5 are numeric.
- A decimal is placed after the third digit (e.g., 250.00 for diabetes mellitus).

## Example formats:

- 3-digit category: 250 (Diabetes mellitus).
- 4-digit subcategory: 250.0 (Diabetes mellitus without mention of complication).
- 5-digit subclassification: 250.00 (Type II diabetes mellitus without mention of complication, not stated as uncontrolled).

## Procedure codes:

- Always numeric.
- 2 digits before the decimal, and up to 2 digits after.
- Example: 45.23 (Colonoscopic polypectomy).

# 2. V and E Codes

## V codes:

- Used for encounters not due to disease/injury (e.g., preventive care, history, aftercare).
- Example: V70.0 (Routine medical examination).

## E codes:

- External cause of injury/poisoning (e.g., accident, homicide, adverse drug effect).

- Example: E880.9 (Accidental fall from unspecified stairs/steps).

- Must be used in addition to a primary diagnosis code.

# 3. Decimal Rules

- Always place the decimal after the third digit.

- Example: 486 → 486 (Pneumonia, organism unspecified).

- If more digits are required: 486.0 (if a subclassification existed).

# 4. Use of Placeholders

- Unlike ICD-10, ICD-9-CM does not use the “X” placeholder. Codes are written only with the digits required.

# 5. Sequence of Codes

- Primary (first-listed) code: the main reason for the encounter.

- Secondary codes: describe coexisting conditions or contributing factors.

- E-codes (external causes) are sequenced after the primary diagnosis.

# Example:

A patient visits for treatment of a femur fracture after a fall at home:

820.8 – Fracture of unspecified part of neck of femur

E888.9 – Unspecified fall
